# Bilateral Distal Biceps Tendinopathy — 12-Week Loading Program

**Case context:** Right arm symptomatic several years, load-dependent, waxing and waning. Left arm symptomatic six months. Pain localized between distal biceps and antecubital/medial elbow, provoked by resisted supinated elbow flexion and heavy pressing. No pop, no bulge, delayed-onset pattern.

**Working assumption:** Chronic tendinosis, bilateral. Degenerative rather than inflammatory. Treatment is progressive mechanical loading.

> **Before starting:** The bilateral pattern and multi-year history are the two features that justify an in-person exam first — to grade the tendon, exclude a partial tear that would change the progression, and screen for systemic contributors (fluoroquinolone exposure, statins, corticosteroids, diabetes/impaired glucose tolerance, thyroid dysfunction). This program is written to be handed to a physio or sports med physician and modified, not run blind.

---

## The Governing Rule

Everything below is gated by one feedback signal.

| Timepoint | Acceptable | Too much |
|---|---|---|
| **During the set** | Up to 4/10 pain | Above 5/10, or sharp/stabbing |
| **Immediately after** | Any level, settling within 2 hours | Escalating |
| **Next morning (24h)** | Same as or better than baseline | Worse than baseline |

The 24-hour response is the one that decides. Pain during loading is expected and is not damage. Pain that is worse the following morning means the dose was too high — repeat the previous week rather than progressing.

Both arms get the same program. Train them at independent loads — the right arm will likely tolerate less. Never let the better arm set the dose for the worse one.

---

## Phase 1 — Isometrics (Weeks 1–2)

**Purpose:** Reduce pain, begin restoring tendon load tolerance without range-of-motion stress.

### Exercise: Isometric Elbow Flexion Hold

- Seated, upper arm against your side, elbow bent to **90°**
- Forearm in **neutral** (thumb up) if supinated position is provocative; move to supinated (palm up) once tolerable
- Push up against an immovable resistance — underside of a desk, a rack pin, a fixed bar, or your other hand
- Build tension over 2 seconds, then hold steady

**Dosage**

| Variable | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Effort | ~70% of maximum |
| Hold | 45 seconds |
| Reps | 5 |
| Rest between reps | 2 minutes |
| Frequency | 2× daily (morning and evening), every day |

### Secondary: Isometric Supination Hold

- Elbow at 90°, hold a hammer or a dumbbell gripped at one end
- Rotate toward palm-up until you feel resistance, hold static against gravity
- 30 seconds × 4, once daily

### Progression criteria to Phase 2

Move on when **all** are true:
- Morning baseline pain reduced from starting point
- 45-second holds tolerated at ≤3/10
- No next-morning flare for 4 consecutive days

If not met at 2 weeks, stay in Phase 1 for another week. This is common and not a setback.

---

## Phase 2 — Heavy Slow Resistance (Weeks 3–12)

**Purpose:** The main intervention. Progressive tendon loading drives collagen remodeling.

**Schedule:** 3× per week, non-consecutive days.

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat | Sun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Session A** | rest / legs | **Session B** | rest / legs | **Session C** | rest | rest |

Sessions A, B, and C are the same exercises. The 72-hour minimum between loading the same tissue is not negotiable.

### Tempo

**3 seconds up, 3 seconds down.** No pause, no bounce, no swing. A set of 10 reps takes a full minute. If you cannot control the 3-second lowering, the weight is too heavy — this is the most common error and it wastes the entire session.

### Warm-up (every session)

1. 5 min general — bike, rower, or brisk walk
2. Elbow flexion/extension through full range, no load, 2 × 15
3. Forearm pronation/supination, no load, 2 × 15
4. One warm-up set of the first exercise at ~50% of working load, full tempo

### Session Structure

**1. Incline Dumbbell Curl** — 3s/3s tempo
The incline (45–60°) puts the biceps on stretch at the bottom, which is where the loading stimulus matters most. Start neutral grip if needed, transition to supinated by week 5.

**2. Supinated Barbell or EZ-Bar Curl** — 3s/3s tempo
EZ-bar for weeks 3–6, straight bar from week 7 if tolerated. The straight bar's full supination is the target position — you need to rebuild it, not avoid it permanently.

**3. Loaded Supination ("Screwdriver")** — 3s/3s tempo
Elbow at 90° and pinned to your side. Hold a dumbbell by one end so the weight hangs to one side. Rotate from palm-down to palm-up. This isolates the supination function of the distal biceps, which is the specific thing that hurts.

### Load Progression

| Weeks | Sets × Reps | Load target | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3–4 | 4 × 15 | Your 15-rep max | 2 min |
| 5–6 | 4 × 12 | Your 12-rep max | 2–3 min |
| 7–8 | 4 × 10 | Your 10-rep max | 3 min |
| 9–10 | 4 × 8 | Your 8-rep max | 3 min |
| 11–12 | 4 × 6 | Your 6-rep max | 3 min |

"Rep max" means the last rep is genuinely hard but the tempo holds and pain stays ≤4/10. Not to failure.

**Weekly load increase:** roughly 5%, only if the 24-hour rule was clean all three sessions the prior week. If it wasn't, repeat the week at the same load.

### The rest of your training during Phase 2

| Body part | Status |
|---|---|
| **Legs** | Unrestricted. Train normally. |
| **Shoulders** | Unrestricted, avoiding deep-stretch positions. |
| **Back / pulling** | Continue, but use neutral or pronated grips. Drop supinated rows and chin-ups. Reduce load ~30% initially, rebuild over the block. |
| **Chest / pressing** | Continue at reduced load (start ~50–60%) with **restricted depth** — stop the dumbbell descent level with the chest, not below. Rebuild toward previous loads across Phase 3. |
| **Direct arm work** | Nothing beyond the three prescribed exercises. No additional curl variations, no training to failure on elbow flexion. |

## Phase 3 — Return to Loading (Week 13 onward)

**Purpose:** Rebuild toward previous training loads and establish permanent maintenance.

### Weeks 13–20

- Reintroduce heavy pressing progressively — add back ~10% load per week, restore full depth only after two clean weeks at full load
- Reintroduce supinated pulling (chin-ups, supinated rows) at low load, progress the same way
- Keep **one** heavy slow resistance session per week: 3 × 6, full 3s/3s tempo

### Week 20 onward — Maintenance

One HSR session weekly, more or less indefinitely. This is the part people skip, and skipping it is the single most common reason chronic tendinopathy recurs. It costs about ten minutes.

Ongoing programming changes worth keeping permanently:
- EZ-bar or hammer curls as the default for heavy elbow flexion work; straight-bar supinated curls as accessory work at moderate load
- Capped dumbbell press depth on heavy sets
- 72 hours between heavy sessions loading the same tissue
- No training to failure on elbow flexion

## Flare Protocol

A flare is 24-hour pain clearly worse than baseline, or a return of pain in daily activities.

1. **Do not stop entirely.** Drop back to Phase 1 isometrics — 5 × 45s, twice daily.
2. **Hold there 3–5 days**, until morning baseline returns.
3. **Resume Phase 2 at 70% of the load** you flared on.
4. **Rebuild over 2 weeks** to where you were.

Expect two or three flares across the twelve weeks. They are part of the normal course and do not mean the program failed. A flare that does not settle within a week, or that comes with new weakness, needs an exam rather than another cycle of this.

## Tracking Log

Record after every session. Three numbers, ten seconds.

| Date | Phase/Week | Load L | Load R | Pain during (0–10) | Pain next AM vs. baseline (better / same / worse) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

The next-morning column is the one that drives every decision in this program. Without it you are guessing.

## Timeline and Expectations

| Marker | Typical |
|---|---|
| First noticeable reduction in daily pain | 3–5 weeks |
| Meaningful strength return | 8–12 weeks |
| Return to previous training loads | 4–6 months |
| Right arm (multi-year) | Longer end of every range above |

Progress is non-linear. A plateau of two to three weeks mid-block is normal. A plateau of six to eight weeks with no change is the point to seek imaging.

---

## What Not To Do

- **Rest alone.** Reliably fails in chronic tendinopathy. The tendon is under-conditioned, not inflamed.
- **Prolonged NSAIDs.** May blunt the adaptive response to loading. Short courses for acute flares only.
- **Corticosteroid injection.** Short-term pain relief, worse long-term outcomes, and elevated rupture risk in load-bearing tendons.
- **Permanently avoiding supination.** Avoidance means the tendon never adapts to the position that provokes it. The provoking movement is the rehabilitation, scaled appropriately.
- **Stretching into pain.** Compressive stretching of an irritable tendon tends to aggravate it.

---

## Red Flags — Stop and Get Seen

- Sudden pop, sharp tearing sensation, or visible change in muscle shape
- Bruising through the antecubital fossa
- Marked weakness in supination compared to the other side
- Numbness or tingling into the forearm or hand
- Pain that no longer varies with load, or night pain
- Flare that does not settle within one week of returning to isometrics
